            <h3><i class="icon-rubric-dark"><span class="dp-icon-content" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></i>Unit Objectives</h3>
            <p>This lecture covers managed (lifecycle) nodes, ROS 2 nodes whose execution is governed by a state machine, giving deterministic startup, shutdown, and error recovery. Lifecycle nodes let you bring up a complex system in a controlled order and are widely used in production-grade ROS 2 software. The lecture builds on the coordinate frame material from Lecture 13.</p>
            <p class="whitespace-normal break-words">By the end of Lecture 14, students will be able to:</p>
            <ul class="[&amp;:not(:last-child)_ul]:pb-1 [&amp;:not(:last-child)_ol]:pb-1 list-disc space-y-1.5 pl-7">
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Explain why managed nodes exist and when to use them</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Describe the lifecycle state machine and its primary states (Unconfigured, Inactive, Active, Finalized)</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Implement a lifecycle node by inheriting from <code>rclcpp_lifecycle::LifecycleNode</code></li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Use the transition callbacks (<code>on_configure</code>, <code>on_activate</code>, <code>on_deactivate</code>, <code>on_cleanup</code>, <code>on_shutdown</code>)</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Understand <code>LifecyclePublisher</code> behavior across states</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Manage lifecycle nodes using <code>ros2 lifecycle</code> CLI commands and programmatic transitions</li>
            </ul>
