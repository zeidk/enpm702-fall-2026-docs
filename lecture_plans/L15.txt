            <h3><i class="icon-rubric-dark"><span class="dp-icon-content" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></i>Unit Objectives</h3>
            <p>This is the final lecture in the course, a capstone that ties together everything from the ROS 2 lectures. Rather than introducing a new ROS 2 feature, it shows how nodes, topics, services, actions, parameters, launch files, TF2, and lifecycle nodes compose into an autonomy architecture, a standard way of organizing perception, decision-making, and control. It also shows where Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning fit into a robotic system.</p>
            <p class="whitespace-normal break-words">By the end of Lecture 15, students will be able to:</p>
            <ul class="[&amp;:not(:last-child)_ul]:pb-1 [&amp;:not(:last-child)_ol]:pb-1 list-disc space-y-1.5 pl-7">
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Describe the <strong>Sense-Plan-Act</strong> paradigm and the autonomy cycle</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Map an autonomy architecture onto ROS 2 nodes and communication patterns</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Identify the perception, decision, and control layers in a robotic system</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Explain <strong>where AI and ML fit</strong> and why the decision layer's implementation is interchangeable</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Recognize how trained models are integrated for inference in C++ (OpenCV DNN, ONNX Runtime, LibTorch), conceptually</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Distinguish reactive, deliberative, and hybrid architectures, and name common ROS 2 frameworks (Nav2, BehaviorTree.CPP)</li>
            </ul>
