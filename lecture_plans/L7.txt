            <h3><i class="icon-rubric-dark"><span class="dp-icon-content" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></i>Unit Objectives</h3>
            <p>This lecture introduces move semantics and smart pointers, two foundational C++11 features that together eliminate the most common memory management bugs. It begins with value categories (lvalues, prvalues, xvalues), rvalue references, and <code>std::move</code>, then explores the three smart pointer types: <code>std::unique_ptr</code> (exclusive ownership), <code>std::shared_ptr</code> (shared ownership), and <code>std::weak_ptr</code> (non-owning observation).</p>
            <p class="whitespace-normal break-words">By the end of Lecture 7, students will be able to:</p>
            <ul class="[&amp;:not(:last-child)_ul]:pb-1 [&amp;:not(:last-child)_ol]:pb-1 list-disc space-y-1.5 pl-7">
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words"><strong>Understand</strong> move semantics and the differences between <em>lvalue</em>, <em>rvalue</em>, and <em>xvalue</em></li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words"><strong>Understand</strong> the different types of smart pointers and their use cases</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words"><strong>Use</strong> <code>std::unique_ptr</code> to express exclusive ownership with move-only semantics</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words"><strong>Use</strong> <code>std::shared_ptr</code> and its reference-counted control block for shared ownership</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words"><strong>Use</strong> <code>std::weak_ptr</code> to observe shared resources without affecting their lifetime</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words"><strong>Apply</strong> idiomatic patterns for passing smart pointers to and returning them from functions</li>
            </ul>
