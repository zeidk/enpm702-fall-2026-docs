            <h3><i class="icon-rubric-dark"><span class="dp-icon-content" style="display: none;">&nbsp;</span></i>Unit Objectives</h3>
            <p>This lecture covers coordinate frame management with TF2, the ROS 2 framework for tracking and transforming between the many reference frames on a robot (its base, each sensor, and the world). TF2 is essential whenever a robot has multiple sensors and links, and it underpins the spatial reasoning used throughout the autonomy stack. The companion topic, lifecycle nodes, is covered in Lecture 14.</p>
            <p class="whitespace-normal break-words">By the end of Lecture 13, students will be able to:</p>
            <ul class="[&amp;:not(:last-child)_ul]:pb-1 [&amp;:not(:last-child)_ol]:pb-1 list-disc space-y-1.5 pl-7">
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Understand what coordinate frames are and why they are essential in robotics</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Describe the TF2 frame tree and standard frame conventions</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Use the <code>TransformStamped</code> message to represent a transform</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Broadcast static and dynamic transforms with TF2</li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Listen for transforms and look them up with <code>lookupTransform()</code></li>
                <li class="whitespace-normal break-words">Handle transform exceptions and debug frames with <code>view_frames</code>, <code>tf2_echo</code>, and RViz2</li>
            </ul>
